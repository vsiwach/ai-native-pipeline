01

Product definition

Product: an OpenAI-compatible endpoint platform for open-weight and customer-tuned models — serverless, dedicated, and in-customer-cloud (BYOC) — running on disaggregated, mixed-vendor fleets (NVIDIA + AMD today; Qualcomm pools post-close) that make Modular the structural low-cost serve.

The conversion machinery is certified migration: shadow a customer’s route from a frontier API or a competitor’s endpoint onto a Modular endpoint, certify quality parity against the customer’s own evaluation sets, promote with instant rollback.

The retention machinery is the perimeter: adapters, evals, context, and tuned checkpoints accumulate inside the customer’s deployment, compounding switching costs in our favor.

The compounding machinery is the agentic pipeline: agents generate in the factory, the harness verifies, only signed kernels reach the field — making every new model and every new chip serveable in days.

The enterprise landing machinery is Fleet Maximizer: recover the utilization customers already waste on hardware they already own — KV-aware routing plus workload-specialized compilation in their VPC, priced as a share of harness-verified savings.

Frontier access: customer-owned keys, pass-through at zero markup, keys in customer KMS; the platform sees metadata, never payloads. We monetize the serving, the certification, and the ledger — never resold tokens.

Commercial ladder: self-serve serverless (funnel) → committed dedicated capacity (marquee AI-natives, 2026) → BYOC/dedicated enterprise contracts (2027) → edge license (post-close, gated).

Supporting metrics: tokens/week served; committed-capacity utilization; certified route wins; $/Mtok leadership per model class in the public harness; day-0 coverage of top model families; enterprise perimeter deployments.

Goals and non-goals

GOALS

(1) Independently proven cost leadership on decode-heavy serving; (2) 3–5 marquee accounts on committed capacity with public reference numbers; (3) certified migration as the repeatable route-winning motion, trajectory-granular for agents; (4) enterprise conversion on proof + perimeter with compliance at GA; (5) the agentic pipeline compressing time-to-economics on every new model and chip; (6) neutrality structural and contractual.

NON-GOALS

Token resale; standalone router or control-plane sales; peak-NVIDIA speed races; post-training as a standalone business (tuning ships only on routes we serve); building gateway mindshare from zero (adopt compatible semantics); homegrown billing (Stripe-metered); prefill-heavy workloads as wedge targets; growth targets before proof exists.

The MVP: next 30 days on the reference implementation

Phase 0 defines the MVP surface; these five items make it real. Each maps to a committed design contract in ai-native-pipeline.

1 · Bake-off kit (F0.3): route config + harness profile + eval-custody contract packaged so a marquee prospect can run their own traffic against a Modular endpoint in under a week. This is the single highest-leverage artifact in the plan.

2 · ./dev bench (F0.2): $/Mtok at fixed SLO for three workload profiles against any adapter (MAX, vLLM, hosted endpoints); publish the first reproducible numbers; open the MI355X-repro and head-model-parity workstreams.

3 · Committed-capacity ledger (F1.1): per-pool commit bands, utilization, and $/Mtok rollups on /v1/costs — the term sheet’s technical backing.

4 · Migration skeleton (F1.2 + F1.3): shadow mode + a signed certification record binding eval-set hash + model build + route config; one frontier pass-through adapter (customer-owned key) + one open-model adapter.

5 · Hygiene & groundwork: LICENSE (Apache 2.0), OTel hooks, Stripe metering spike, and an egress-class field on every route (the context-firewall seed).

Open questions

Marquee target list — which 5 accounts, what committed volumes clear the utilization floor, and who owns the relationships through the acquisition quiet period?

Neutrality made visible — the guarantees are contractually secured; what publication cadence (placement records, governance reports) makes them legible to a skeptical enterprise buyer fastest?

Trajectory-level parity on agent routes — the least-proven claim in the plan; the three Phase-1 design partners answer it, and capacity spend does not scale before they do.

Lab partnership economics — what do model labs need (compute credits, co-marketing, revenue share?) to release multi-silicon day-0 through us, and does frontier-checkpoint custody create legal exposure?

Agent benchmark arbitration — who runs the Mojo-vs-Triton/Helion agent suite so it isn’t dismissed as our homework; candidate: an existing independent harness with external rerun rights.

Capacity funding — Modular P&L or Qualcomm strategic budget for the committed-burst neocloud contracts, and at what dollar cap?

Fleet Maximizer gain-share accounting — what savings attribution does the customer’s finance team accept (harness-verified tokens/sec/GPU vs their invoice), and what share converts them fastest without leaving margin on the table?

Kernel provenance in security review — what signing, SBOM, and reproducibility evidence do enterprise security teams require before agent-authored (factory-verified) kernels are admitted to their VPCs?
